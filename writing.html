Email to the Manager

Role:

  • As a liaison representative for the [X] team, I am writing to...
  • Being the main point of contact between our customers and management, I...
  • As someone who is privy to (with inside access to) the concerns our clients have raised, I feel obliged to...
  • Having dealt with these customers firsthand, I am well-placed to bring this matter to your attention.
  • As a front-line/customer-facing member of the [X] department, I...
  • In my capacity as a junior member of the team, I...
  • In our department mailbox, I discovered a few complaint emails recently.

Customers:

  • Our customer-service email box has been inundated with complaints in recent weeks.
  • Our hotline has been ringing off the hook, with a steady stream of disgruntled customers.
  • A growing number of clients have aired their grievances over [policy X].
  • Customers have taken to social media to voice their dissatisfaction with [policy X].
  • Several long-standing clients have been let down by [policy X].
  • Our policy on [X] has been branded unfair/unjust/discriminatory by a section of our customer base.
  • Our customers aired their grievances against our no-exchange/no-refund policy, and expressed their wish to have their money back or product exchanged for their wrong-sized or mismatched garments.

Company:

  • I understand that [policy X] is no doubt well-intentioned, implemented with the company's best interests at heart.
  • I appreciate the rationale behind [policy X], as it is designed to protect our bottom line.
  • This policy stems from the organisation's need to safeguard its core interests.
  • I recognise that [policy X] aligns with our overarching mission and objectives.
  • I am well aware that such measures were not introduced without good reason.
  • I understand that the no-exchange/no-refund policy is in-place for the sake of our company's efficient operations.

Concede → Pivot:

  • While [policy X] is well-intentioned, ...
  • I appreciate the rationale behind [policy X]; that said...
  • Much as [policy X] serves our interests, ...
  • I fully understand why [policy X] was introduced. However, ...
  • [Policy X] no doubt has its merits, yet ...
  • Nonetheless, I hope you can kindly consider bringing up the matter of changing the policy ... for the following reasons.

Client 1st:

  • That said, our reputation for unbeatable customer service must remain intact.
  • Even so, we have always placed our customers at the top of our priority list.
  • Our company has long stayed firmly customer-centric/customer-oriented/client-first in its approach.
  • If we are to weather this situation, we must keep customer satisfaction at the forefront of our operations.
  • Ultimately, retaining our customers' trust matters as much as protecting our short-term interests.
  • Therefore, the change of policy can no doubt lead to a win-win situation for our customers and our company.

Outline:

  • In this letter, I will + [keyword]
  • The purpose/intent of this letter is to + [keyword]
  • With this letter, I aim to/hope to + [keyword]
  • I am writing with a view to + [keyword]

Outline keywords:

  • examine/probe into/get to the heart of [X]
  • unearth/uncover the root cause of/reveal the underlying factors behind [X]
  • propose actionable/feasible/well-considered suggestions regarding [X]
  • assess/gauge the implications of [X]
  • In this letter, I will examine why this policy is backfiring and propose changes that protect both our customers and our bottom line.

Sum:

  • To sum up, while [policy X] was introduced with good reason, in its current form it is costing us valuable customer goodwill.
  • In conclusion, balancing our business objectives with customer satisfaction is no longer optional but essential.
  • All things considered, a modest adjustment to [policy X] would serve our customers and the company alike.
  • To sum up, it is opined that the change to allow our customers to exchange clothing items or get a refund can enhance our company's image and expand our customer base.

Ask:

  • I urge you to reconsider [policy X] so that our customers feel heard.
  • It is my sincere hope that we can review [policy X] before further damage is done.
  • I would be grateful for the chance to discuss possible adjustments at your earliest convenience.

Win:

  • I firmly believe that with a timely revision, we can retain our customers without compromising the company's interests.
  • With our combined efforts, I am confident we can turn this situation around and emerge stronger.
  • Let us work together to protect both our customers and our hard-earned reputation.

Social Media Influencers

Skills:

  • Don't just talk about cons. Talk about pros also.
  • There are five different angles that can be talked about: social, moral, knowledge, obsession and look (SMKOL).

Social:

Helps - you find your people.

  • A shy teen find a niche community where they finally belong.
  • Distant friends stay close across any distance.
  • Shared anecdotes online can spark real friendships.

Harms - closeness gets thinner.

  • Hundreds of online "friends" rarely include one who'd show up at midnight.
  • A like is not friendship - it is a fleeting trap.
  • Because screens feel social, they quietly isolate us from the people beside us.
  • I, as a former victim of bullying, simply couldn't let people online be bullied and antagonised.
  • They belittle others to feel compensated for their own insecurities.

Morals:

Helps - good examples exist.

  • The best influencers steer teens toward kindness and real role models.
  • Some use symbiotic/win-win brand deals to fund genuinely useful work.

Harms - honesty goes up for sale.

  • Influencers monetize our trust - every post can hide a vested interest.
  • A paid recommendation is not advice - it is an advert.
  • The more they flaunt wealth, the more teens tie self-worth to money.
  • Because attention pays, honesty gets compromised for views.
  • Social media influencers are more capable than traditional media advertisements to instil beliefs into our heads, hence making us believe easily what they claim.

Knowledge:

Helps - a free education in your pocket.

  • Anyone can learn coding, cooking, or a language for free.
  • Real experts reach thousands who'd never open their book.
  • Curiosity that once needed a library is now a tap away.

Harms - unchecked claims spread.

  • A confident caption is not a credible fact.
  • They are not experts - they are entertainers with a mic.
  • Only the social media-savvy spot it - the rest absorb myths as truth.
  • The more a claim is shared, the truer it starts to feel.
  • Hardly could their posts and stories be checked by multitudinous boards as they are not put under strict regulations, thus giving rise to the opportunity for influencers and KOLs to give false data on health products' effects.
  • Social media influencers could easily give false information to the public compared to traditional media advertisements.
  • In my video, I often include astonishing facts that teenagers may find interesting and illuminating.

Obsession:

Healthy passion - a deep dive that grows you.

  • A shared obsession - art, coding, a cause - sparks real skill and belonging.
  • Following creators who love what you love can turn a hobby into a craft.
  • The right role model can sway a teen toward a lasting passion.

Harmful addiction - hooked on the feed.

  • Every ping is a tiny reward, keeping us hooked like a slot machine.
  • Chasing likes ties self-worth to a number.
  • Hours of scrolling for happiness leave us emptier than before.
  • Do not let others validate you, as only you can put validation to your name.
  • When I was about to publish it on Instagram, my heart sank - what if I get criticised and ostracised/marginalized by people online?

Looks:

Some push back.

  • Body-positive creators challenge narrow beauty standards.
  • Seeing real, unedited bodies helps some feel normal.

Impossible standards.

  • Overnight virality turns one filtered face into the look every chases.
  • Filters and surgery normalise faces no one is born with.
  • "Perfect body" pressure fuels dieting and eating disorders.
  • Chasing a look you can't reach breeds constant insecurity.
  • It is perfectly normal and fine to feel insecure in the era where KOLs seem to get every single thing right.
  • A KOL named Blackruby sold a drug that claimed to help lose weight. Teenage girls flocked for the products and a few ended up being sent to hospital, as the drugs contain substances that wreak havoc on their health.

Examples

Example: Fast Fashion no-refund policy (stance: revise the policy)

Task: You work in the sales team of an online clothing retailer. Customers have flooded you with complaints about the company's strict no-exchange, no-refund policy. Write an email to the sales manager explaining why the policy should change, suggesting changes, and addressing how to stop customers abusing the new policy.

Intro:

Dear Ms Chan,

As a customer liaison for our sales team, I am privy to the steady stream of complaints that has reached us since the no-refund policy took effect. Our inbox has been inundated with messages from disgruntled shoppers, many of whom feel trapped by purchases they cannot return. I fully understand that this policy is well-intentioned - it was designed to safeguard the company against losses from frivolous returns. That said, our reputation for customer-first service must remain intact if we are to retain these shoppers long term. In this email, I will examine why the policy is backfiring and propose changes that protect both our customers and our bottom line.

Conclusion:

To sum up, while the no-refund policy was introduced with good reason, in its current form it is costing us valuable customer goodwill. I urge you to consider a limited-window exchange option so that customers feel heard without exposing us to abuse. I firmly believe that with this timely revision, we can retain our shoppers without compromising the company's interests. Let us work together to protect both our customers and our hard-earned reputation.

Example: Are influencers more trustworthy than ads? (stance: YES)

Task: Many young people trust an influencer's word over a TV or magazine ad. Do you think influencers really are more trustworthy? Argue your view.

M (+)

An influencer is not the mouthpiece of a big company, so their view feels like a single, unbiased one.

S (+)

The friend-like bond makes their word sway us more personally than any corporate slogan.

M (+)

They often show real, hands-on use of a product, not a scripted studio claim.

[close]

To many young people, that makes an influencer's word more believable than an ad.

Example: Good or bad for young people? (stance: NEGATIVE)

Task: Influencers shape how millions of teenagers think, feel, and spend. Overall, are they a good or a bad influence on young people? Argue one side.

O (-)

Endless highlight reels breed comparison and anxiety, with self-worth counted in likes.

L (-)

Overnight virality turns filtered, "perfect" bodies into a standard no real teenager can meet.

K (-)

Unchecked claims spread misinformation faster than any correction.

S (-)

Hours on a screen quietly replace the real friendships that matter most.

[close]

The harm to a young person's mind, mood, body, and bonds far outweighs the good.

Example: Your environmental blog → letter to a UN climate conference (genre: letter)

Task: You run an Instagram blog raising awareness about climate change. Write to a UN climate conference explaining why you started it and what you learned.

K (+)

I started it to turn social media into a classroom, making climate science simple enough for any teenager to act on.

S (+)

Running it, I learned the power of a shared niche - thousands of strangers became a movement no single voice could build alone.

M (+)

I found that an honest, unpaid platform can sway opinion precisely because no brand or profit sits behind it.

[close]

Used well, social media gave an ordinary student a real voice in a global cause.

Example: Should teenagers aim to become influencers? (stance: NO)

Task: Being an influencer looks like a dream job. Should young people actually chase it as a career? Argue your view.

M (-)

Chasing sponsorships quietly monetises your honesty, turning it into a product for sale.

O (-)

Tying your worth to likes and views is a fragile, anxious way to live.

L (-)

The pressure to look camera-perfect every day wears down real wellbeing.

[close]

A life built on the feed costs more of your mind and character than it pays.

Model Essay

Model Essay: Group Work

Question: Group work is common in the workplace and its value has long been recognised. However, group work may be unpopular among workers. Write an essay discussing the challenges of group work in the workplace and ways to overcome those challenges.

Tip:

essay = article, no special format

Tip 2:

neat handwriting is very important also

Framework:

Intro: Praise, then Pivot

Praise group work's accept value, flip with a single "Yet...", end with a rhetorical question + a three-challenge roadmap sentence.

Body 1: [Challenge 1] Unequal Workload - the "free-riders":

  • low productivity
  • slacking off
  • being lazy

Body 2: [Challenge 2] Contrasting Visions:

  • lacking mission

Body 3: [Fix 1] Assigned Ownership:

  • motivate
  • assign missions

Conclusion: Concede, Then Promise

Concede the challenges are real, then close on the promise - group work turned "milder, more just and collaborative" - sealed with the essay's only exclamation mark.

Content:

productivity, recognition, mission, incentive, ecosystem (PRIME)

Model Essay (943 words):

Group work acts as an indispensable method of gathering ideas from diversified colleagues, with their minds brimming with distinct thoughts and opinions, it has long been acknowledged as a conducive way of contributing information and ideas to the workplace. Yet the efficiency of groupwork has become contentious as workers are plagued by the challenges of working with others in their team. What are the challenges of group work that has arose lately in the workplace? This essay will underscore three pivotal challenges and provide corresponding solutions.

First and foremost, group work encompasses unequal distribution of duties to the workers. Nowadays, we are no stranger to the terms "slackers" and "free-riders" which indicate those who contribute minimal work to their assigned tasks and those who highly depend on their groupmates on producing quality work. These workers also prowl in the workplace, unleashing their laziness and irresponsibility to their unsuspecting colleagues. This is substantiated by a report from the University of Michigan, which conducted a survey on American employees and the results show a staggering 80% of employees having to confront "slackers" and "free-riders" during group work in the office. It also stated that respondents feel dissatisfied, incensed and frustrated when it comes to encountering such incompetent colleagues in the workplace. "Never again, would I volunteer to be in a team project," asserted Mr Smith who worked in the large conglomerate - United Projects, which is renowned for operating team projects on scientific research. "It is nice to collect different opinions from my fellow colleagues, but I could spare those who bask in the praise and accolade from the leaders when they contributed nothing to the project," he exclaimed. It is no doubt a headache for diligent workers who aspire all colleagues to work wholeheartedly on the project, and when a worker is inundated with the tasks of a teammate who is not willing to contribute, it can be seen that group work may pose as a challenge and become unfavourable to workers.

Additionally, group work amasses a myriad of opinions, and when colleagues uphold different stances, relationships could be a trouble to maintain. A syndicate of workers though with the same objective, may hold contrasting visions to execute a plan. Ms Young, an acclaimed marine scientist of the famous "Bring Our Freshwater Turtles Back to Shore" conservation program grimly accounted the brainstorming process of their research: "There was a variety of ideas on how we could kickstart our research; some suggested a field research, some suggested a research on only bringing turtles in captive back to the wild. We had limited time to work on the project and it was disastrous when we couldn't reach a consensus and make ends meet. Incompatibility in group work is really the worst thing." These heated arguments proved deleterious to office relationships as workers hold dissatisfaction, annoyance and frustration towards their counterparts, resulting in infeasible work performance and outcome. A questionnaire published by government investigation company Joy in Office, Inc., counted over 15,000 failed team projects amongst outstanding commercial companies, with 85% of the failed attempts owing to incompatibility amongst groupmates. This implies that tense work relationships resulted by group work in the office acts as a hindrance to the performance of companies. With incompatibility being a major factor to dissatisfaction in the workplace, it can be seen why group work remains a challenge to workers and they are hesitant to participate in more team projects.

Last but not least, group work could be inflexible to workers and hinders them from working to the best of their ability. As employees come together to discuss on their group work, meetings, documents, communication methods have to be agreed on unanimously to ensure efficiency. A survey conducted by Harvard University states that inflexibility is the main contributer to the unpopularity of group work in the workplace in 2024. "As the digital world becomes more pervasive, people tend to think that they can work efficiently despite geographical boundaries through online means. Though a laudable attempt, the effort to group a number of workers to meet up on a certain date, at a certain time, in different places is an exorbitant labour to some." Mr Smith, a sociology professor from Harvard University contends. A natural phenomenon observed nowadays would be that people would like to work in a flexible way, whenever and wherever they want, and with group work, it is hard to do that. It is seen that group work is a less prominent option for workers as they want to work faster and more efficiently with technology; group work requires too much planning and is not worth the wait to work any more.

Despite the challenges of group work in the workplace, there are a few ways to simply tackle the difficulties. Firstly, by creating task lists and requiring workers to state the person-in-charge of each assigned part of duty. It can eliminate the risk of prospective "slackers" and "free-riders" from emerging, as they need to harbour the responsibility of a part that everyone knows that they need to take part in.

Secondly, workers can make use of online resources such as polls and brainstorming websites to overlook all the workers' opinions. These resources offer anonymous identities for workers to voice out their opinions without the fear of being overlooked or not considered. These techniques help in ensuring just and fair treatment to all opinions and ideas can be presented in a more clear way to reduce hostility in critical opinions.

In conclusion, there are challenges to group work but by attempting these alternative solutions in team projects, group work in the office may be milder and more just and collaborative!
