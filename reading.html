Reading

Skills to help you answer reading questions.

Skills Examples

Skills

Skill 1: Copy or don't copy?

  1. Copy if the question is in the format of "according to...", "based on..." or "using the info...".
  2. DO NOT copy if the question is in the format of "imply...".

Skill 2: Find the clue

  1. The question keyword(s) can be found in the passage.
  2. Synonym(s) of the question keyword(s) can be found in the passage.
  3. Identify if the question is positive or negative, then look for positive/negative elements in the passage.

Skill 3: Look forwards or backwards?

  1. effect because cause
  2. cause as a result effect
  3. something can cause

Skill 4: Reference Questions

Reference questions are in the format of "... replace ..." or "... refer to ...".

This is an existing concept - the answer is usually found by looking backwards at the sentence(s) before the referenced word.

Skill 5: Spot the "Two"

Pay attention to prefixes, "and", "with", "duo", "both", "pair".

Prefixes that signal "two" or "together":

  • bi- (bicycle, bilingual, bilateral)
  • di- (dichotomy, dioxide)
  • duo- (duopoly, duologue)
  • twi- (twin, twilight)
  • ambi- (ambidextrous - using both hands)
  • amphi- (amphibian - living in both water and land)
  • co- (cooperate, coexist - together)
  • syn-/sym- (synergy, symphony - together)
  • inter- (interact, interconnect - between)

Why this is crucial:

When a question contains these signals, the answer almost always requires TWO elements, not one. For example, if the question asks about a "pair" of reasons or uses "both", you must find two answers in the passage. Students who miss this signal often give only one answer and lose marks. Spotting the "two" signal early tells you exactly how many points to look for.

Examples

Example 1 2012 DSE P1 A Q16

All very nice, but come on, girls - seriously - have you seen the latest iPad and Xbox Kinect? How can you compete with that?

'Parents want pastimes that will keep their children away from the TV and computer,' says Pat. 'They're trying to get back into more interactive things now. The way a child behaves reflects how his parents treat him. Activities like this that get families together can only be a good thing.'

Polar Board Games & Cafe is in Phase 2 of the Crystal Design Centre on Ram Indra-Ekamai Road and is open weekdays from 1 pm to 9 pm and weekends from 11 am to 10 pm. Find out more at (083) 133 0743 or www.polarplaygames.com.

Question: According to Pat, why do parents want to keep their children away from the TV and computer?

Skill 1:

The question is in the format of "according to...", so our answer should be copied from the passage.

Skill 2:

Question keywords: keep their children away from the TV and computer

We can see the exact keywords at the start of paragraph 2, in the sentence 'Parents want pastimes that will keep their children away from the TV and computer'.

Skill 3:

This is the starting sentence of paragraph 2, so the answer is unlikely to be in the previous paragraph/sentences. If we look forward, we will be able to identify the answer, which is 'They're trying to get back into more interactive things now.'

Complete correct answer:

This is because parents want their children to get back into more interactive things now.

Wrong answers:

It is because parents want...

Reason: Always use 'This is because' as the beginning of your answer.

This is because they are trying to get back into...

Reason: 'they' can refer to both the parents and the children, which would be unclear.

Example 2 2017 DSE P1 A Q07

Recycling has been relentlessly promoted as a goal in and of itself: a public and private virtue that is indoctrinated in students from kindergarten through university. As a result, otherwise well-informed and educated people have no idea of the relative costs and benefits.

Question: According to the passage, why are people ill-informed about recycling?

Skill 1:

The question is in the format of 'according to...', so our answer should be copied from the passage.

Skill 2:

Question keyword: ill-informed

The ending sentence of the paragraph 'otherwise well-informed and educated people have no idea of the relative costs and benefits' implies that people are 'ill-informed about recycling'.

Some students may not know the word 'ill-informed'. There is another way to solve the problem:

We know that the word 'ill-informed' is negative, 'have no idea' is also negative. Thus, we understand that 'ill-informed about recycling' and the ending sentence of the paragraph are connected.

Skill 3:

In the beginning of the ending sentence of the paragraph, we can see 'As a result'. Therefore, the cause of people being 'ill-informed about recycling' is behind 'As a result'.

Complete correct answer:

This is because a public and private virtue that is indoctrinated in students from kindergarten through university.

Example 3 2014 DSE P1 A Q05

Rachel pulled at his shirt sleeve. "Daddy, who's Karl?"

"We worked together at CommaTech, then he got a great job at Future Industries. I applied to work there, but they never called back."

The robot beeped. "Pardon, Winston Sinclair. Will you hear the apology?"

"Uh, okay."

Question: Who does 'they' refer to in 'but they never called back'?

Skill 4:

Look backwards.

Complete correct answer:

members from Future Industries

Example 4 2015 DSE P1 A Q04

You started, like so many other Westerners in Korea, teaching English. When did you begin writing about Korea and why?

When I joined The Economist I thought, "Eventually I'd like to write a book about Korea because nobody else is really doing it."

Why weren't they?

Korea is a bit off the radar for most people in Western countries. In the 1980s Japan was the big story and people pay attention to China now because of its huge population and market. Korea has fallen in between these two countries.

Question: Who does "they" refer to in "Why weren't they?"

Skill 4:

Look backwards.

Skill 2:

The sentence "Why weren't they?" is negative, so is "nobody else is doing it". These two sentences are connected.

Note:

"they" can refer to both people and objects.

Westerners in Korea → plural, so this is the answer

A detailed explanation regarding the answer:

The author started teaching English, just like many other westerners in Korea. However, westerners in Korea are not writing a book about Korea. The subheading asks "Why weren't they?", referring to westerners in Korea.

Complete correct answer:

westerners in Korea

Example 5 2019 DSE P1 A Q13

No, I think clearly there are many situations where messes are just entirely dysfunctional, and distractions just make it impossible to get stuff done. My arguement in the book is not that mess is always good, distraction is always good, or there's no situation that can't be improved by adding a little chaos. I don't believe that at all. What I do believe is that we found that our organizational systems can be so effective, and they make us feel so comfortable that we take them from situations where they work extremely well, and then we start trying to apply them in situations where they're completely inappropriate.

Question: What does 'them' in 'and then we start trying to apply them in situations where they're completely inappropriate' refer to?

Skill 4:

Look backwards.

Complete correct answer:

organizational systems

Question: Are organizational systems functional or dysfunctional?

'We start trying to apply them (organizational systems) in situations where they're completely inappropriate.'

Complete correct answer:

dysfunctional
