    Reading

    Skills to help you answer reading questions.

    Basic Skills DSE Skills Examples

    Basic Skills

    Understanding question constraints

    Pay close attention to the specific instructions in the question. They dictate exactly how you should provide your answer. Before you read the passage, scan the question for these keywords:

    • Quantity: Are you asked to write ONE word, TWO words, or NO MORE THAN THREE words? Exceeding the word limit will result in a loss of marks.
    • Form: Does the question ask for a 'word' or a 'phrase'? A phrase is a group of words. Make sure your answer matches the required grammatical form. If it asks for a word, extracting a phrase will not be accepted.
    • Target Paragraph: Look for instructions like "taken from paragraphs 6 to 7". You must locate the answer only within these specified paragraphs. An answer from elsewhere, even if it contains the correct information, will not score marks.
    • Grammar: Pay attention to phrases like "Your answers must be grammatically correct." If the question states "taken from the passage", you usually need to copy the exact word. However, sometimes a word needs to change from singular to plural or verb tense to fit the new sentence. Always read the context carefully.

    Skimming

    To understand the topic of a text, you do not need to read every word. Skimming is useful when answering questions that require you to identify the theme or overall views expressed in a text.

    • Look at the title, the subtitles as well as the first and last paragraphs.
    • Read the first and last sentences of the other paragraphs.
    • Think about the overall meaning of the text.

    Thematic questions

    A thematic question is about the overall theme of a text (what the whole text is about). A thematic question may ask you:

    • about the writer's main message or conclusion
    • to choose an alternative title for the text
    • to identify the type of text or the target audience
    • to identify the source of the text

    The last 3 points are mostly asked in MC format.

    Multiple-choice questions

    • Read the question and study all the options first carefully. Try to make predictions about which options are possible answers and which can be eliminated straightaway.
    • Skim the title, subtitle, the first and last paragraphs as well as the first and the last sentences of the other paragraphs to find the part of the text that is related to the question.

    Understanding the writer's purpose

    You have learnt how to use skimming to get an idea of the general theme or gist of a text. Once you understand the theme of a text, you can probably identify its purpose. Texts are written for many different purposes, such as:

    • to argue
    • to criticize
    • to entertain
    • to evaluate
    • to explore
    • to express
    • to inform (provide information)
    • to instruct (teach)
    • to persuade
    • to satirize (show that someone/something is wrong, similar to criticize)

    The first five wordings are mainly for challenging someone/something.

    Please be reminded that a text may also have more than one intended purpose.

    DSE Skills

    Skill 1: Copy or don't copy?

    1. Copy if the question is in the format of "according to...", "based on...", "using the info...", "with reference to...", "in the writer's opinion..." or "list...".
    2. DO NOT copy if the question is in the format of "imply...".
    3. If the question does not satisfy the formats above, in most situations, we assume that the answer should be copied from the passage. However, there are a few situations where we should not copy from the passage even if the question does not satisfy the formats above.

    Skill 2: Find the clue

    1. The question keyword(s) can be found in the passage.
    2. Synonym(s) of the question keyword(s) can be found in the passage.
    3. Identify if the question is positive or negative, then look for positive/negative elements in the passage.

    Skill 3: Look forwards or backwards?

    1. effect because cause
    2. cause as a result effect
    3. something can cause

    Skill 4: Reference Questions

    Reference questions are in the format of "... replace ..." or "... refer to ...".

    This is an existing concept - the answer is usually found by looking backwards at the sentence(s) before the referenced word.

    Skill 5: Spot the "Two"

    Many questions require two answers, not one. Missing this signal means losing marks on an otherwise correct response. There are four categories of clues to watch for:

    1. Prefixes that signal "two" or "together":

    • bi- (bicycle, bilingual, bilateral)
    • di- (dichotomy, dioxide)
    • duo- (duopoly, duologue)
    • twi- (twin, twilight)
    • ambi- (ambidextrous - using both hands)
    • amphi- (amphibian - living in both water and land)
    • co- (cooperate, coexist - together)
    • syn-/sym- (synergy, symphony - together)
    • inter- (interact, interconnect - between)

    2. Conjunctions and connectives:

    • "and" - the most common: "A and B", "X, Y and Z"
    • "with" - "together with", "along with", "with the help of"
    • "as well as" - introduces an additional element
    • "in addition to" / "besides" / "moreover"
    • "not only... but also..." - two-part structure
    • "either... or..." / "neither... nor..." - paired alternatives

    3. Quantifiers and determiners:

    • "both" - "both X and Y"
    • "pair" / "duo" / "couple"
    • "two" / "the two" / "twice"
    • "a set of" / "a series of"
    • "among others" - hints at more than one

    4. Punctuation and structure:

    • Comma + "and" - "X, Y and Z" signals three (or more) items
    • Colon introducing a list - "The reasons are: A, B..."
    • Paired quotation marks - "X" and "Y" mentioned separately
    • Numbered or lettered items - (i) ... (ii) ... in the question
    • Parallel structures - two consecutive clauses with matching grammar

    Why this is crucial:

    When a question contains any of these signals, the answer almost always requires TWO elements, not one. For example, if the question asks about a "pair" of reasons or uses "both", you must find two answers in the passage. Students who miss this signal often give only one answer and lose marks. Spotting the "two" signal early tells you exactly how many points to look for.

    Skill 6: Answering "What does 'This' refer to?"

    When a question asks what "This" refers to, "This" often refers to a whole idea or situation in the previous sentence, not just a single noun. In that case, do not answer with only a noun phrase. Begin your answer with "The fact that..." and complete it with a clause that states the idea.

    1. Look backwards to the sentence(s) before "This".
    2. Identify the whole idea that "This" stands for.
    3. Turn that idea into a clause after "The fact that...".

    Skill 7: Noun answers — use sentence structures to locate the exact answer

    If the part of speech of the answer is a noun, we can find the exact answer by searching for the following sentence structures:

    1. adj. + n.
    2. V. + ing / to + v.
    3. that + SVO

    Use these structures to narrow down where the noun answer is likely to appear in the passage, then copy the exact wording required by the question. When the answer is a verb phrase instead, use the same idea: decide the part of speech first, then locate the matching structure.

    Skill 8: Questions about contradictions

    Questions like "What is the contradiction between A and B?" ask you to locate where the passage sets A and B against each other.

    The formula to look for in the passage is:

    A but B

    • Check whether A and B are positive or negative respectively — a contradiction usually pairs a positive with a negative (or two opposites).
    • A common example is a wide perspective vs a narrow perspective.
    • Signal words to look for: "but", "yet", "however", "although", "despite", "whereas".

    Skill 9: Questions about connections

    Questions like "What is the connection between A and B?" ask you to locate the sentence that links A and B together.

    The formula to look for in the passage is:

    A and B

    • Check the properties / implied meaning of A and B — how does the passage relate them?
    • Look for the sentence where both A and B appear together (or via synonyms), then state the relationship the passage describes.

    Skill 10: Questions about main purpose

    Questions asking about the main purpose of a paragraph or the whole text are answered by looking for the topic sentence — usually the first (or sometimes the last) sentence of the paragraph.

    Express the purpose using one of these forms:

    1. to + v. (e.g. to show, to prove)
    2. V. + ing
    3. SVO

    Read the topic sentence carefully and convert it into one of the above structures to state the purpose precisely.

    Example 1 2012 DSE P1 A Q16

    All very nice, but come on, girls - seriously - have you seen the latest iPad and Xbox Kinect? How can you compete with that?

    'Parents want pastimes that will keep their children away from the TV and computer,' says Pat. 'They're trying to get back into more interactive things now. The way a child behaves reflects how his parents treat him. Activities like this that get families together can only be a good thing.'

    Polar Board Games & Cafe is in Phase 2 of the Crystal Design Centre on Ram Indra-Ekamai Road and is open weekdays from 1 pm to 9 pm and weekends from 11 am to 10 pm. Find out more at (083) 133 0743 or www.polarplaygames.com.

    Question: According to Pat, why do parents want to keep their children away from the TV and computer?

    Skill 1:

    The question is in the format of "according to...", so our answer should be copied from the passage.

    Skill 2:

    Question keywords: keep their children away from the TV and computer

    We can see the exact keywords at the start of paragraph 2, in the sentence 'Parents want pastimes that will keep their children away from the TV and computer'.

    Skill 3:

    This is the starting sentence of paragraph 2, so the answer is unlikely to be in the previous paragraph/sentences. If we look forward, we will be able to identify the answer, which is 'They're trying to get back into more interactive things now.'

    Complete correct answer:

    This is because parents want their children to get back into more interactive things now.

    Wrong answers:

    It is because parents want...

    Reason: Always use 'This is because' as the beginning of your answer.

    This is because they are trying to get back into...

    Reason: 'they' can refer to both the parents and the children, which would be unclear.

    Example 2 2017 DSE P1 A Q07

    Recycling has been relentlessly promoted as a goal in and of itself: a public and private virtue that is indoctrinated in students from kindergarten through university. As a result, otherwise well-informed and educated people have no idea of the relative costs and benefits.

    Question: According to the passage, why are people ill-informed about recycling?

    Skill 1:

    The question is in the format of 'according to...', so our answer should be copied from the passage.

    Skill 2:

    Question keyword: ill-informed

    The ending sentence of the paragraph 'otherwise well-informed and educated people have no idea of the relative costs and benefits' implies that people are 'ill-informed about recycling'.

    Some students may not know the word 'ill-informed'. There is another way to solve the problem:

    We know that the word 'ill-informed' is negative, 'have no idea' is also negative. Thus, we understand that 'ill-informed about recycling' and the ending sentence of the paragraph are connected.

    Skill 3:

    In the beginning of the ending sentence of the paragraph, we can see 'As a result'. Therefore, the cause of people being 'ill-informed about recycling' is behind 'As a result'.

    Complete correct answer:

    This is because a public and private virtue that is indoctrinated in students from kindergarten through university.

    Example 6 2021 DSE P1 A Q27

    [6] Central to our stewardship program, however, is employee engagement. Last year, we had about 60 'Go Green Ambassadors' but less than 50 percent of our employees attended our sustainability events. We have three measures to improve employee engagement.

    [7] All new staff need to attend training sessions to increase awareness of environmental issues facing Southeast Asia as well as Singapore, with a focus on solutions that our company promotes to solve these problems. Additionally, we should provide rewards for different teams and departments when they meet their key performance indicators. Finally, we plan to host innovation contests for our employees to promote solutions that are unique and applicable to their specific departments.

    Question: Complete the following summary by writing ONE word taken from paragraphs 6 to 7 in each blank below. Your answers must be grammatically correct. (4 marks)

    The (i) ________ of our staff members is the key to our success in environmental goals. For this reason, we would like to implement three initiatives that aim to increase participation. To raise their awareness of all Star Resort environmental policies, (ii) ________ will be mandatory for all new employees. We know that all employees like to receive (iii) ________ when they reach their targets. So these will be provided as our second initiative. Also, employee ownership of initiatives is a motivator so we can hold competitions to encourage employees' (iv) ________ in finding new solutions.

    Skill 0: Analyze the constraints (Basic Skill)

    Before reading the passage, note the constraints from the question:

    • Quantity: ONE word per blank.
    • Source: Taken from paragraphs 6 to 7 (the answer must be found here).
    • Requirement: Answers must be grammatically correct.

    Skill 1: Copy or don't copy?

    The question states "taken from paragraphs 6 to 7", so our answers should be copied directly from the passage.

    Skill 2 & 3: Find the clues and look forwards/backwards

    (i) The ________ of our staff members is the key to our success...

    • Clue: "staff members" is a synonym for "employees". "Key to our success" relates to being "Central to our stewardship program".
    • Location: Paragraph 6, first sentence: "Central to our stewardship program, however, is employee engagement."
    • Answer: engagement (from "employee engagement").

    (ii) To raise their awareness of all Star Resort environmental policies, ________ will be mandatory for all new employees.

    • Clue: "raise their awareness" is a synonym for "increase awareness". "mandatory for all new employees" matches "All new staff need to attend".
    • Location: Paragraph 7, first sentence: "All new staff need to attend training sessions to increase awareness..."
    • Answer: training (Note: We choose "training" instead of "training sessions" because the question requires ONE word).

    (iii) We know that all employees like to receive ________ when they reach their targets.

    • Clue: "reach their targets" is a synonym for "meet their key performance indicators". "receive" relates to "provide".
    • Location: Paragraph 7, second sentence: "Additionally, we should provide rewards for different teams and departments when they meet their key performance indicators."
    • Answer: rewards.

    (iv) ...hold competitions to encourage employees' ________ in finding new solutions.

    • Clue: "competitions" relates to "contests". "encourage employees'" relates to "for our employees to promote".
    • Location: Paragraph 7, third sentence: "Finally, we plan to host innovation contests for our employees to promote solutions..."
    • Answer: innovation.

    Complete correct answers:

    (i) engagement

    (ii) training

    (iii) rewards

    (iv) innovation
