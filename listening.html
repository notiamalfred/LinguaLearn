Symbols & Abbreviations

Use symbols:

  • and: &, +
  • or: /
  • dollars/money: $
  • leads to/results in: →/←
  • means/equals: =
  • more (than): >
  • less (than): <
  • increase: in↑
  • decrease: de↓
  • raise: ra↑
  • rise: ri↑
  • reduce: re↓
  • drop: dr↓
  • per cent: %
  • with: w/
  • without: w/o

Common abbreviations:

  • for example: e.g.
  • that is (to say): i.e.
  • in the morning: a.m.
  • in the afternoon/evening: p.m.
  • number: no.

Shorten words by the first syllable:

  • minute: min
  • English: Eng
  • second: sec

Shorten words by using the first syllable + first letter of the second syllable:

  • society: soc
  • restaurant: rest
  • community: com-ity
  • company: com-any

Shorten words by leaving out some or all of the vowels:

  • group: grp
  • hour: hr
  • ticket: tkt

Basic Skills

Preparing for listening

In the HKDSE Paper 3 exam, you will be given time to study the Part A tasks before the recording is played. Use this time to:

  • study the situation, the rubric and the answer sheet to get a good idea of the topic.
  • think about what you expect to hear in the recording.
  • study the answer sheet and try to guess what answer, or type of answer, is required for each blank.

Listening for keywords

When you are listening to a recording, it is important to pay attention to keywords that will help you complete a task.

Before you listen:

  • study the task and try to guess what information you will need to complete it. On the answer sheet, underline keywords to help you focus on what to listen for.

While you listen:

  • listen for the keywords you have underlined.
  • listen for other words and phrases that signal the specific information required by the task. (e.g. because for reasons, lives at for a home address, dollars for prices/costs/fees).

Date Changes

Use a strikethrough to mark down date changes.

Example:

Recording: the prize giving ceremony will be held on the twenty-first of April on Monday ... oh I'm sorry, it should be on the twenty-second.

What you should mark down: 21st April on Monday +1

Writing Down Years

Recording: nineteen thirty-nine to nineteen eighty-seven

What you should write down: 1939-87

What you should not write down: 1939-1987

DSE Skills

External Email — Analysis + Formatting

5 of the most popular types of writing in DSE Paper 3

  1. email
  2. report
  3. article
  4. speech
  5. info page

Intro

  1. 客 (+ve)
    • Thank you for your previous email...
  2. 景 (background) (WH)
    • We understand...
  3. 動
    • I am writing this email...

Conclusion

  1. 總 (summarizing)
  2. 動
    • Please + v.
  3. 員 (完)
    • Thank you + e.g. partnership

Formatting

Email Subject
Salutation
  • Formal salutation: Dear Sir/Madam/Name of the recipient, / To whom this may concern, (if name is not known)
  • Informal salutation: Hello Name of recipient, / Hi Name of recipient
Introduction

Greeting:

  • I hope that this email find you well
  • I hope you are well
  • I hope you are having a great day
  • I hope all is well with you

Thank you (客):

  • Thank you for all the work you have done on X
  • Thank you for your earlier response.
  • Thank you for your timely reply.
  • Your previous response is much appreciated.
  • I am appreciative of your detailed response.

Goal to inform (動):

I am writing to...

  • provide information about X
  • inform you about X
  • provide more details about X
  • confirm with you some details about X
  • ensure you are aware of X

Goal - to request:

I am writing to...

  • request that + SVO / request your help with X
  • kindly ask you to + V
  • seek your assistance with X
  • ask if you would be willing to + V
  • We apologise once again for X and sincerely hope you can accept our Y and enjoy Z with us.
Conclusion

Summary:

  • All in all, the details of X have been summarized above.
  • In short, these are the main points regarding X.
  • In summary, the key details regarding X have been outlined above.
  • To recap, the main points about X have been covered.
  • To wrap up, these are the crucial details of X.

Action required:

  • I eagerly anticipate receiving your reply regarding X
  • I would appreciate your timely response
  • I look forward to hearing back from you at your earliest convenience.
  • It would be appreciated if you could + V

Deadline:

  • This is a gentle reminder that the deadline is [date]
  • Please ensure that the final draft is submitted to us before [date]
  • We kindly request that you complete all work before [date]
  • Due to our own time restraints, please get back to us by [date]

Thank you (員):

  • Thank you for taking the time to read this email
  • Thank you in advance
  • Thank you for your attention
  • Thank you for your consideration
  • I appreciate you taking the time to work on this

Enquiries:

  • Should you have any queries, please feel free to contact us/me.
  • If you have any doubts or questions, please do not hesitate get in touch with me/us.
  • Should there be any questions, please feel free to let me know.
  • Should you have any questions, I would be more than happy to answer them.

Positive wish (offering a line of "positive wish"):

  • I look forward to our continued partnership.
  • I have high hopes for our continued partnership.
  • It is our pleasure to work and collaborate with you/your company.
  • We value our partnership and look forward to its growth.

Formal close: Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely,

Informal close: Best Regards, / Regards, / Best,

Language

Mutual Interests
  • I feel that X would be mutually beneficial for both of us.
  • It is in both of our interests that + SVO
  • We both stand to gain from X [e.g. this project]
  • Our partnership will yield significant benefits for both parties
Question
  • Would it be preferable if...?
  • Could you please confirm if...?
  • Do you think it would be beneficial to...?
  • Can you let me know if ... works for you?
If
  • It would be better if...
  • It would be advantageous if...
  • If you could + V, it would greatly help.
  • If we could ensure that + SVO, it would be ideal.
Kindly
  • I would kindly suggest/recommend...
  • I kindly request that you...
  • Kindly note that...
  • Kindly consider...
  • We genuinely hope that you can continue X with us, as we value each and every customer in the highest regard.

Examples

Example 1 — Crossroads Game Cafe competition

Task: Your company is running a competition for the game Escape to Hong Kong Island at Crossroads Game Cafe, a local gaming cafe, so people can try the game out. Write an email to the cafe to update them on what has been decided and what you need. Use your email exchange with Tommy as a starting point.

Intro:

  1. 客 (+ve)
    • Thank you for your previous email.
  2. 景 (-ve) (WH)
    • We understand that a competition for the game Escape to Hong Kong Island will be run at Crossroads Game Cafe.
  3. 動
    • I am writing this email to update you on what has been decided and what we need.

Full Intro:

We appreciate your timely reply to our request (客) for hosting our upcoming game competition at Crossroads Game Cafe (景). Before we proceed, we would like to finalize and confirm a few details. (動)

Example 2 — Feedback to the artist (Ryan Au)

Task: Write an email to Ryan Au, the artist, with feedback on the artwork samples he sent for Escape to Hong Kong Island. Using the questionnaire results and your staff's comments (and Eric Park's points from the podcast), summarise what works and what should be changed in the final artwork. Keep a polite tone - try not to upset the artist.

Intro:

  1. 客 (+ve)
    • Thank you for your previous email and your submitted artwork.
  2. 景 (-ve) (WH)
    • We have read your feedback on the artwork samples you sent for Escape to Hong Kong Island.
  3. 動
    • I am writing this email to summarise what works and what should be changed in the final artwork.

Conclusion:

  1. 總
  2. 動
  3. 員 (完)

Full Intro:

I would like to begin by thank you deeply for the work you have put in so far on the game. We deeply appreciate your effort. (客) Having reviewed your work, we are pleased with many aspects of the designs (景); however, we would kindly ask for some parts to be changed. (動)

Full Conclusion:

In short, these changes will be crucial to our final artwork. (總) Please be aware that the deadline for the project is quickly approaching, so the changes will need to be finalised by the 27th of this month. (動) For any questions, please feel free to let me know. Once again, thank you for your effort on the project. (員)
