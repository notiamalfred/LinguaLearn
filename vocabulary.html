Vocabulary

Detailed explanations of useful words and phrases.

liaison

Part of speech: noun

Pronunciation: /liˈeɪ.zɒn/

Meaning: a person who acts as a link between two or more groups or organizations, helping them communicate and work together.

Example: As a liaison representative for the team, I am writing to share our customers' concerns.

Synonyms: link, contact, go-between, intermediary, coordinator

Antonyms: stranger, outsider, opponent

who is privy to

Part of speech: phrase (adjective + preposition)

Pronunciation: /ˈprɪv.i tuː/

Meaning: having special or secret knowledge of something; being allowed to know information that others do not have access to.

Example: As someone who is privy to the concerns our clients have raised, I feel obliged to bring this matter to your attention.

Synonyms: aware of, informed about, in the know, having inside access to

Antonyms: unaware of, ignorant of, uninformed about

inundated

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈɪn.ʌn.deɪ.tɪd/

Meaning: overwhelmed or flooded with a very large amount of something, such as work, messages, or requests, so that it is hard to deal with them all.

Example: Our customer-service email box has been inundated with complaints in recent weeks.

Synonyms: flooded, swamped, overwhelmed, deluged, buried

Antonyms: empty, idle, unburdened

flooded

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈflʌd.ɪd/

Meaning: filled or overwhelmed with a very large number or amount of something, as if covered by water.

Example: The support team was flooded with emails after the announcement.

Synonyms: inundated, swamped, deluged, overwhelmed

Antonyms: empty, drained, scarce

swamped

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /swɒmpt/

Meaning: having far too much work or too many things to deal with at one time.

Example: We are swamped with orders during the holiday season.

Synonyms: inundated, flooded, overloaded, buried, overwhelmed

Antonyms: idle, free, unburdened

deluged

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈdel.juːdʒd/

Meaning: flooded with a huge number of things, such as messages or requests, all arriving at once.

Example: The company was deluged with complaints after the price increase.

Synonyms: inundated, flooded, swamped, overwhelmed

Antonyms: empty, drained, unburdened

disgruntled

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /dɪsˈɡrʌn.təld/

Meaning: angry or dissatisfied, especially because of unfair treatment or unmet expectations.

Example: Our hotline has been ringing off the hook, with a steady stream of disgruntled customers.

Synonyms: dissatisfied, unhappy, annoyed, displeased, resentful

Antonyms: satisfied, content, pleased, happy

dissatisfied

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /dɪsˈsæt.ɪs.faɪd/

Meaning: not happy or content with something; feeling that something is not good enough.

Example: Several dissatisfied customers demanded a full refund.

Synonyms: disgruntled, displeased, unhappy, discontented, aggrieved

Antonyms: satisfied, content, pleased

aggrieved

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /əˈɡriːvd/

Meaning: feeling angry or upset because of unfair treatment; feeling that one has been wronged.

Example: The aggrieved customers demanded an apology from the company.

Synonyms: wronged, resentful, disgruntled, offended, dissatisfied

Antonyms: pleased, content, satisfied

displeased

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /dɪsˈpliːzd/

Meaning: annoyed or unhappy about something; not satisfied with a situation.

Example: The manager was displeased with the drop in sales.

Synonyms: dissatisfied, annoyed, unhappy, disgruntled, irritated

Antonyms: pleased, satisfied, content

hinge

Part of speech: verb (hinge on/upon)

Pronunciation: /hɪndʒ/

Meaning: to depend entirely on something; to be decided or determined by a particular factor.

Example: The success of the new policy hinges on how well our staff communicate it to customers.

Synonyms: depend on, rest on, be contingent on, revolve around

Antonyms: be independent of, be unrelated to

win-win

Part of speech: adjective (also used as a noun)

Pronunciation: /ˌwɪnˈwɪn/

Meaning: a situation or solution in which all parties involved benefit; everyone gains something positive and nobody loses.

Example: Offering exchanges for wrong-sized garments is a win-win solution for both the customers and the company.

Synonyms: mutually beneficial, advantageous to all, no-lose

Antonyms: lose-lose, zero-sum

well-intentioned

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˌwel ɪnˈten.ʃənd/

Meaning: having or showing good, kind, or helpful intentions, even if the result is not always successful.

Example: I understand that the policy is no doubt well-intentioned, implemented with the company's best interests at heart.

Synonyms: well-meaning, well-meant, kind-hearted, sincere

Antonyms: ill-intentioned, malicious, malevolent

safeguard

Part of speech: verb (also used as a noun)

Pronunciation: /ˈseɪf.ɡɑːd/

Meaning: to protect something from harm or damage; to keep something safe from danger or loss.

Example: This policy stems from the organisation's need to safeguard its core interests.

Synonyms: protect, defend, shield, preserve, secure

Antonyms: endanger, jeopardise, expose, threaten

protect

Part of speech: verb

Pronunciation: /prəˈtekt/

Meaning: to keep someone or something safe from harm, damage, or danger.

Example: The policy is designed to protect the company's bottom line.

Synonyms: safeguard, shield, defend, preserve, secure

Antonyms: endanger, harm, expose, attack

shield

Part of speech: verb (also used as a noun)

Pronunciation: /ʃiːld/

Meaning: to protect someone or something from danger, harm, or unpleasantness.

Example: The new measures shield the company from financial losses.

Synonyms: protect, safeguard, defend, guard, shelter

Antonyms: expose, endanger, attack

preserve

Part of speech: verb

Pronunciation: /prɪˈzɜːv/

Meaning: to keep something in its original state or in good condition; to maintain something so it is not lost or damaged.

Example: We must preserve our reputation for excellent customer service.

Synonyms: protect, maintain, safeguard, keep, conserve

Antonyms: destroy, damage, ruin, neglect

intact

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ɪnˈtækt/

Meaning: complete and not damaged or changed in any way; remaining whole and unharmed.

Example: That said, our reputation for unbeatable customer service must remain intact.

Synonyms: unbroken, undamaged, whole, untouched, preserved

Antonyms: damaged, broken, destroyed, ruined

undamaged

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ʌnˈdæm.ɪdʒd/

Meaning: not harmed, spoiled, or broken in any way.

Example: Despite the storm, the warehouse and its stock remained undamaged.

Synonyms: intact, unharmed, unbroken, whole, untouched

Antonyms: damaged, broken, harmed, ruined

unharmed

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ʌnˈhɑːmd/

Meaning: not hurt or damaged in any way; safe.

Example: Our reputation emerged from the crisis unharmed.

Synonyms: intact, undamaged, safe, unhurt, unscathed

Antonyms: harmed, hurt, injured, damaged

untarnished

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ʌnˈtɑː.nɪʃt/

Meaning: not spoiled or damaged; keeping a good reputation or image with no stain or blemish.

Example: The company has maintained an untarnished record of customer service.

Synonyms: spotless, unblemished, intact, pristine, unstained

Antonyms: tarnished, stained, blemished, spoiled

niche

Part of speech: noun (also used as an adjective)

Pronunciation: /niːʃ/

Meaning: a small, specialised segment of a market, interest, or community that appeals to a particular group of people.

Example: A shy teen finds a niche community where they finally belong.

Synonyms: specialised, targeted, specific, corner, segment

Antonyms: mainstream, general, mass-market

anecdotes

Part of speech: noun (plural of anecdote)

Pronunciation: /ˈæn.ɪk.dəʊts/

Meaning: short, interesting, or amusing stories about real people or events, often shared in conversation.

Example: Shared anecdotes online can spark real friendships.

Synonyms: stories, tales, accounts, recollections

Antonyms: facts, data, evidence

fleeting

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈfliː.tɪŋ/

Meaning: lasting for only a very short time; passing quickly.

Example: A like is not friendship - it is a fleeting trap.

Synonyms: brief, momentary, short-lived, transient, passing

Antonyms: lasting, permanent, enduring, eternal

symbiotic

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˌsɪm.baɪˈɒt.ɪk/

Meaning: involving a relationship between two parties that benefits both of them.

Example: Some use symbiotic brand deals to fund genuinely useful work.

Synonyms: mutually beneficial, interdependent, win-win, cooperative

Antonyms: parasitic, one-sided, independent

monetize

Part of speech: verb

Pronunciation: /ˈmʌn.ɪ.taɪz/

Meaning: to earn money from something, especially by turning an asset, audience, or activity into a source of income.

Example: Influencers monetize our trust - every post can hide a vested interest.

Synonyms: commercialise, capitalise on, profit from, cash in on

Antonyms: give away, donate, devalue

due diligence

Part of speech: noun phrase

Pronunciation: /ˌdjuː ˈdɪl.ɪ.dʒəns/

Meaning: the careful investigation or research done before making a decision or trusting a claim, to make sure it is reliable.

Example: Before believing an influencer's health claims, consumers should do their due diligence.

Synonyms: careful checking, investigation, verification, research

Antonyms: negligence, carelessness, recklessness

savvy

Part of speech: adjective (also used as a noun)

Pronunciation: /ˈsæv.i/

Meaning: having practical knowledge and good judgement about a particular subject; shrewd and well-informed.

Example: Only the media-savvy spot it - the rest absorb myths as truth.

Synonyms: shrewd, knowledgeable, astute, streetwise, sharp

Antonyms: naive, clueless, ignorant, gullible

multitudinous

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˌmʌl.tɪˈtjuː.dɪ.nəs/

Meaning: very numerous; existing in great numbers.

Example: Hardly could their posts be checked by multitudinous boards as they are not put under strict regulations.

Synonyms: numerous, countless, many, abundant, myriad

Antonyms: few, scarce, rare, limited

sway

Part of speech: verb

Pronunciation: /sweɪ/

Meaning: to influence or persuade someone to change their opinion, decision, or behaviour.

Example: The right role model can sway a teen toward a lasting passion.

Synonyms: influence, persuade, convince, affect, win over

Antonyms: dissuade, deter, discourage

criticised

Part of speech: verb (past tense of criticise)

Pronunciation: /ˈkrɪt.ɪ.saɪzd/

Meaning: to express disapproval of someone or something by pointing out faults or problems.

Example: What if I get criticised and ostracised by people online?

Synonyms: condemned, attacked, disapproved of, faulted, slammed

Antonyms: praised, commended, applauded

ostracised

Part of speech: verb (past tense of ostracise)

Pronunciation: /ˈɒs.trə.saɪzd/

Meaning: excluded from a group or society; deliberately ignored or rejected by others.

Example: What if I get criticised and ostracised by people online?

Synonyms: excluded, shunned, rejected, outcast, isolated

Antonyms: welcomed, included, embraced, accepted

marginalized

Part of speech: adjective (also used as a verb)

Pronunciation: /ˈmɑː.dʒɪ.nə.laɪzd/

Meaning: treated as unimportant or pushed to the edge of society; having little power or influence.

Example: Body-positive creators give a voice to marginalized groups who are rarely represented online.

Synonyms: excluded, sidelined, disadvantaged, overlooked, disempowered

Antonyms: included, empowered, central, mainstream

virality

Part of speech: noun

Pronunciation: /ˌvaɪəˈræl.ə.ti/

Meaning: the tendency of content to spread rapidly and widely across the internet, like a virus.

Example: Overnight virality turns one filtered face into the look everyone chases.

Synonyms: rapid spread, popularity, trend, buzz, fame

Antonyms: obscurity, anonymity, unpopularity

extrinsic

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ekˈstrɪn.zɪk/

Meaning: coming from outside rather than from within; motivation driven by external rewards such as money, prizes, or praise.

Example: Extrinsic rewards like bonuses can boost short-term productivity.

Synonyms: external, outside, outward, material

Antonyms: intrinsic, internal, inherent

intrinsic

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ɪnˈtrɪn.zɪk/

Meaning: belonging naturally to something; motivation driven by internal satisfaction, interest, or enjoyment rather than external rewards.

Example: Intrinsic motivation - a genuine love of the work - keeps teams going long after rewards fade.

Synonyms: inherent, internal, natural, built-in, essential

Antonyms: extrinsic, external, acquired

prowl

Part of speech: verb

Pronunciation: /praʊl/

Meaning: to move around quietly and secretly, often looking for an opportunity to take advantage of a situation.

Example: These workers also prowl in the workplace, unleashing their laziness and irresponsibility to their unsuspecting colleagues.

Synonyms: lurk, sneak, creep, skulk, roam

Antonyms: march, stride, work openly

avid

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈæv.ɪd/

Meaning: showing great enthusiasm, interest, or eagerness for something; extremely keen or passionate about an activity or subject.

Example: As an avid fan of baseball, he watches every game.

Synonyms: enthusiastic, passionate, zealous, fervent, eager, keen

Antonyms: indifferent, apathetic, disinterested, unenthusiastic

lucrative

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈluː.krə.tɪv/

Meaning: generating a lot of profit or financial reward; producing substantial wealth or income.

Example: She left teaching for a more lucrative career in finance.

Synonyms: profitable, moneymaking, gainful, remunerative, well-paying

Antonyms: unprofitable, loss-making, nonlucrative

peculiar

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /pɪˈkjuː.li.ər/

Meaning: strange, odd, or unusual in a way that makes something interesting or noticeable; out of the ordinary.

Example: The sky had a peculiar appearance before the storm.

Synonyms: odd, strange, uncommon, unusual, curious, eccentric

Antonyms: common, ordinary, usual, normal, mediocre

impressionable

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ɪmˈpreʃ.ən.ə.bəl/

Meaning: easily influenced, affected, or shaped by external forces such as experiences, people, or ideas; especially used to describe young people whose attitudes and beliefs are still forming.

Example: An impressionable youngster can be greatly changed by his or her experiences.

Synonyms: impressible, susceptible, influenceable, suggestible, receptive, pliant

Antonyms: unimpressionable, resistant, inflexible, unaffected

overrated

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˌəʊ.vəˈreɪ.tɪd/

Meaning: considered better or more important than it actually is; hyped beyond its true worth.

Example: Like many diners, I braced myself for yet another overrated Instagram-bait restaurant.

Synonyms: overhyped, exaggerated, overvalued, overpraised

Antonyms: underrated, underappreciated, overlooked

insipid

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ɪnˈsɪp.ɪd/

Meaning: lacking flavour; bland and dull. Can also describe something lacking interest or vigour.

Example: Skeptics fear walking into X only to find insipid filler dishes priced for the view.

Synonyms: bland, tasteless, flavourless, dull, flat

Antonyms: flavoursome, tasty, savoury, delicious

acclaimed

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /əˈkleɪmd/

Meaning: praised enthusiastically and publicly; widely recognised for excellence.

Example: X has been acclaimed across independent channels, from the Michelin Guide to OpenRice.

Synonyms: celebrated, praised, lauded, recognised, applauded

Antonyms: criticised, condemned, obscure, unknown

praised

Part of speech: verb (past tense of praise)

Pronunciation: /preɪzd/

Meaning: expressed warm approval or admiration for someone or something.

Example: The restaurant has been praised by food critics across the city.

Synonyms: commended, applauded, acclaimed, lauded, complimented

Antonyms: criticised, condemned, blamed, censured

celebrated

Part of speech: adjective (also past tense of celebrate)

Pronunciation: /ˈsel.ɪ.breɪ.tɪd/

Meaning: famous and well-known for a particular quality or achievement.

Example: The restaurant has been celebrated by food critics across the city.

Synonyms: renowned, famous, acclaimed, distinguished, illustrious

Antonyms: unknown, obscure, forgotten, undistinguished

recognised

Part of speech: adjective (also past tense of recognise)

Pronunciation: /ˈrek.əɡ.naɪzd/

Meaning: acknowledged as valid, important, or good; formally accepted.

Example: The restaurant has been recognised by food critics across the city.

Synonyms: acknowledged, accepted, acclaimed, honoured, credited

Antonyms: ignored, overlooked, unacknowledged, dismissed

Naysayers

Part of speech: noun (plural of naysayer)

Pronunciation: /ˈneɪˌseɪ.əz/

Meaning: people who criticise, oppose, or predict negative outcomes; people who say "nay".

Usage note: In argumentative writing, Naysayers is used to introduce a counter-argument — you present the opposing view, then explain why you disagree with it. Often paired with skeptics: naysayers have already decided against something, while skeptics doubt until they see evidence.

Example: Naysayers may counter my stance, insisting that the restaurant is overrated.

Synonyms: pessimists, critics, cynics, doubters, dissenters

Antonyms: believers, supporters, optimists, enthusiasts

signature

Part of speech: adjective (also used as a noun)

Pronunciation: /ˈsɪɡ.nə.tʃər/

Meaning: the dish that a restaurant is best known for; the defining feature that represents a person or brand.

Example: The appetising signature X delivers a depth of flavour that Y rarely attempts.

Synonyms: characteristic, distinctive, hallmark, trademark

Antonyms: generic, ordinary, unspecified

skeptics

Part of speech: noun (plural of skeptic)

Pronunciation: /ˈskep.tɪks/

Meaning: people who doubt or question claims until evidence is provided; those unwilling to accept something without proof.

Usage note: Often paired with Naysayers in counter-argument writing. A skeptic doubts until convinced; a naysayer has already decided against and argues from that position.

Example: Skeptics fear walking into X only to find insipid filler dishes priced for the view.

Synonyms: doubters, disbelievers, cynics, questioners

Antonyms: believers, devotees, enthusiasts, proponents

scrumptious

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈskrʌmp.ʃəs/

Meaning: extremely tasty; delicious.

Example: A deeply scrumptious mushroom-and-tofu dumpling at a cha chaan teng.

Synonyms: delicious, delectable, appetising, flavoursome, mouth-watering

Antonyms: tasteless, insipid, unappetising

appetising

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈæp.ɪ.taɪ.zɪŋ/

Meaning: appealing to the senses and stimulating appetite; looking or smelling appealing.

Example: The appetising signature X delivers a depth of flavour that Y rarely attempts.

Synonyms: appealing, tempting, mouth-watering, scrumptious

Antonyms: unappetising, insipid, repulsive

flavoursome

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈfleɪ.və.səm/

Meaning: full of flavour; having a strong, pleasant taste.

Example: The flavoursome signature X delivers a depth of flavour that Y rarely attempts.

Synonyms: tasty, savoury, rich, full-flavoured

Antonyms: bland, insipid, tasteless

delectable

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /dɪˈlek.tə.bəl/

Meaning: delicious; extremely enjoyable to eat or experience.

Example: The delectable signature X delivers a depth of flavour that Y rarely attempts.

Synonyms: delicious, scrumptious, mouth-watering, delightful

Antonyms: unpalatable, insipid, revolting

seamless

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˈsiːm.ləs/

Meaning: smooth and continuous, with no apparent gaps or interruptions.

Example: The experience was seamless — needs anticipated without a single interruption to the conversation.

Synonyms: smooth, fluid, uninterrupted, flawless

Antonyms: disjointed, jarring, interrupted

palate

Part of speech: noun

Pronunciation: /ˈpæl.ət/

Meaning: the sense of taste; the ability to distinguish flavours.

Extended: Palate recalibration is the process of retraining your sense of taste so that you become more sensitive to subtle flavours — for example, the natural sweetness of a roasted carrot, or the smoky note in a wok-tossed green. It often happens after you step away from heavy, dominant flavours (like pork or beef) and eat lighter, more delicate food for a sustained period.

Example: The biggest gain of the week was not the menu but the palate recalibration.

Synonyms: taste, taste buds

Antonyms: — (no direct antonym)

acquired taste

Part of speech: noun phrase

Pronunciation: /əˈkwaɪəd teɪst/

Meaning: something that is initially unpleasant or strange but becomes enjoyable after repeated exposure.

Example: X presents an acquired taste — it rewards the diner who returns past the first wince.

Synonyms: acquired preference, cultivated taste

Antonyms: natural preference, instant liking

patronised / patronized

Part of speech: verb (past tense of patronise / patronize)

Pronunciation: /ˈpæt.rə.naɪzd/

Meaning: (in a restaurant context) frequented or supported as a customer; regularly visited by paying customers. Also spelled patronized in American English.

Example: X is patronised by celebrities without a single paid cost — organic patronage is the strongest endorsement.

Synonyms: frequented, supported, shopped at, visited

Antonyms: boycotted, avoided, shunned

half-convinced

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˌhɑːf.kənˈvɪnst/

Meaning: only partially persuaded; having doubts; not fully believing something.

Example: I went into the week half-convinced I would be living on insipid vegetable filler.

Synonyms: doubtful, hesitant, uncertain, sceptical

Antonyms: convinced, certain, sure

esoteric

Part of speech: adjective

Pronunciation: /ˌes.əˈter.ɪk/

Meaning: understood by only a small number of people with specialised knowledge; obscure.

Example: The chef's esoteric tasting menu was lost on most diners but celebrated by critics.

Synonyms: obscure, cryptic, arcane, specialised

Antonyms: common, mainstream, accessible, popular
