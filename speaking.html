Example: Bring Your Own Device Policy

Task: In your group, discuss the possibility of introducing a "Bring Your Own Device" policy in your school. You may want to talk about:

  • the benefits and problems of this policy
  • how to prevent such problems
  • in which subjects mobile devices would be most useful for students
  • anything else you think is important

It is NOT recommended to talk about 'anything else you think is important'.

Q1 Pros and Cons:

Topic Sentence Elaboration Example
1. Enhance learning opportunities. Instant access to edu tools like interactive learning apps. Enrich understanding e.g. history and science.
2. Equity and distraction issues. Not all students have high-quality devices. Distraction during class persists. Over half of students use SM during class - disrupting focus.

Q2 Prevention:

Topic Sentence Elaboration Example
1. Provide devices for students who lack so. Ensure equal access to tech and edu resources. 1:1 device initiative - providing loan laptops to students.
2. Implement strict usage policy and tools. Clear guidance on acceptable use to keep students focused. GoGuardian allow teachers to appropriately monitor students.

Q3 Applicable Subjects:

Topic Sentence Elaboration Example
1. STEM subjects including Math and Science. Provide interactive simulations, coding apps for students. Khan Academy and Labster can offer virtual lab exp.
2. Language Arts such as English and Chinese. E-books and audiobooks can provide diverse learning styles. Grammarly can aid with writing assistance and Audible aids with listening.

Group Discussion

Preparation for Discussion

The mastery of several language skills is required to do a good discussion. The skills below will deal with preparation work. In fact, preparation for discussion both orally or in the form of a writing is an on-going process. Students should be reading widely and regularly so as to be able to contribute meaningfully to a discussion on any topics including social issues. It has frequently been remarked in HKEAA Examination Reports that candidates generally perform better if the themes of the passages are related to their daily lives especially those about school matters. It is also suggested that students should expose themselves to the world around them. Reading newspapers, journals and magazines regularly, attending live forums or watching the broadcast on television, browing websites for articles or speeches on hot topics can all help to widen students' knowledge of the bigger world.

Understanding the Passage

Students should read the passage most carefully to understand:

  • the theme of the passage
  • the genre of the passage
  • the discussion task(s)
  • the suggested points for discussion

The discussion task usually comprises two parts, first, discussing the topic itself; second, deciding how to present the points discussed for a specific purpose as in writing a complaint letter, writing an article or preparing a presentation. However, students should not just limit themselves to the suggested points when doing the discussion.

Managing the Discussion

If time is running out and the other students are still talking about the same point, do not try to interrupt them awkardly. Instead, express your opinions regarding to the students' points, and do your best to bridge the conversation from the current point to the next point.

Comprehending the Passage

  • You should first read the title. It might be the summary of the whole passage. It might be a question to raise a query.
  • You should also note the type and genre of the passage. The passage can be any kind of text. This can help you to understand the tone faster.
  • If you discover that there are wrods you don't understand, try to guess. Don't just skip them.
  • If you discover words you can't pronounce, decide if they are crucial. See if you can substitute. If you can't, then try to say those words several times until you are in control of the pronunciation. Don't be scared and panic by words that you can't pronounce.

Taking Notes

  • IMPORTANT: Your notes must be neat and tidy for easy reading. Don't scribble.
  • You are to write down only the main ideas of the passage. Not all names and figures are important.
  • You might make your notes in the form of bullet points, a mind-map or a flow-chart. You can use symbols and abbreviations. Please DO NOT write out a whole essay or copy the whole passage for presentation.
  • You can pratice all forms of note-taking. Reflect on how useful your notes are every time you do your practice. Find out the kind of notes that can help you most.
  • You are also to note down your own ideas regarding the topic. Be original and creative but practical. Don't be over-creative. Taking your role seriously can help you.
